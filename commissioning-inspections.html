ENERGIZED DOES NOT ALWAYS MEAN CORRECT

A New System Can Operate and Still Contain Defects

A newly installed system may energize successfully, communicate with monitoring equipment, and produce power while still containing connection problems, installation defects, module abnormalities, load imbalances, or other conditions that are not apparent during conventional visual inspection.

Thermal inspection adds another layer of verification by observing equipment while it is energized and operating. Abnormal heat patterns can identify areas that deserve additional investigation before project acceptance.

That makes thermography particularly useful near the end of commissioning—when the system is operational, warranties are active, and corrective work is still easier to assign and document.

COMMISSIONING SCOPE

One Inspection Program Across the PV and Electrical System

The strongest commissioning program combines aerial PV thermography with ground-based electrical inspection.

PV Array

Aerial thermal inspection can rapidly evaluate large areas of photovoltaic modules under operating conditions.

  • Module thermal abnormalities
  • Cell and substring hotspots
  • String-level differences
  • Shading and soiling effects
  • Module location documentation

PV Electrical Equipment

Ground inspection can evaluate the balance-of-system equipment that connects the array to the facility electrical system.

  • Combiner boxes
  • Inverters
  • DC disconnects
  • AC disconnects
  • Terminations and conductors

Distribution Equipment

Electrical thermography can be extended downstream into accessible distribution equipment.

  • Switchgear
  • Panelboards
  • Load centers
  • Transformers
  • Breakers, fuses, taps, and splices

COMMISSIONING WORKFLOW

From Construction Completion to Documented Baseline

Thermal commissioning is most useful when it is integrated into project turnover rather than treated as an isolated inspection.

1. Review

Review drawings, equipment schedules, asset lists, project scope, and commissioning requirements.

2. Coordinate

Confirm access, safety requirements, energization status, loading, and environmental conditions.

3. Inspect

Capture thermal and visible imagery of PV and electrical assets under suitable operating conditions.

4. Analyze

Evaluate abnormal thermal patterns and compare similar components, phases, modules, and equipment.

5. Report

Document findings with location, imagery, measurements, priority, and recommended follow-up.

6. Verify

Support corrective action and, where required, re-inspect completed repairs or resolved findings.

STANDARDS-ALIGNED INSPECTION

Commissioning Requires the Right Standard for the Right System

PV THERMOGRAPHY

IEC TS 62446-3

Provides guidance for outdoor infrared thermography of operating photovoltaic modules and plants, including data acquisition, environmental conditions, equipment, analysis, and reporting.

COMMISSIONING

ANSI/NETA ECS

Provides a commissioning framework for electrical power systems and helps support verification that installed systems meet project requirements and intended operation.

ACCEPTANCE

ANSI/NETA ATS

Addresses acceptance testing and inspection used to evaluate newly installed electrical equipment before final acceptance and long-term service.

ThermaVista should describe its services as aligned with or informed by applicable standards unless a specific certification, accreditation, or formal authorization is held.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Commissioning Inspection FAQ

When should the thermal commissioning inspection be performed?

The inspection should generally occur after the system has been energized and can operate under meaningful load. For PV systems, suitable solar irradiance and environmental conditions are also required for useful thermal evaluation.

Does a successful startup mean the system does not need thermal inspection?

No. Equipment may energize and operate while still containing connection issues, load imbalances, module abnormalities, or other conditions that are not apparent during startup or visual inspection.

Can thermal findings be used on a project punch list?

Yes. Thermal findings can be documented with equipment identification, location, visible imagery, infrared imagery, temperature data, and recommended follow-up so they can be assigned and tracked through project closeout.

Can ThermaVista verify repairs after corrective work?

Yes. Re-inspection can be used to document post-repair thermal condition when the equipment can be safely operated under suitable load and inspection conditions.

Is commissioning thermography only for solar projects?

No. Thermography can support commissioning of a wide range of electrical systems, including switchgear, transformers, panelboards, disconnects, distribution equipment, inverters, and other energized electrical assets.

What is the difference between commissioning and maintenance thermography?

Commissioning thermography evaluates newly installed or newly modified equipment and establishes initial condition. Maintenance thermography evaluates equipment already in service and is often used to detect deterioration or compare conditions over time.
