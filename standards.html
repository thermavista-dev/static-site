TECHNICAL FOUNDATION

A Thermal Image Is Evidence. A Proper Inspection Provides Context.

Thermal cameras detect infrared radiation and provide information about surface temperature patterns. But meaningful interpretation also depends on system load, environmental conditions, emissivity, reflected radiation, equipment characteristics, and measurement technique.

Recognized inspection standards help make thermographic observations more consistent, repeatable, and useful for engineering and maintenance decisions.

PHOTOVOLTAIC THERMOGRAPHY

IEC TS 62446-3

Outdoor infrared thermography provides an efficient method for identifying abnormal temperature patterns across operating PV modules and plants.

The technical specification addresses inspection conditions, procedures, equipment, analysis, reporting, and personnel considerations.

WHY IT MATTERS

Reliable PV Inspection Starts With Controlled Conditions

Solar thermal inspection depends on sufficient irradiance and meaningful electrical operation. Wind, clouds, reflections, and viewing geometry can affect temperature measurements and the visibility of thermal anomalies.

  • Irradiance: Sufficient and stable sunlight to support meaningful thermal contrast.
  • Wind: Conditions evaluated to limit convective cooling effects.
  • System operation: Appropriate PV operating conditions during inspection.
  • Viewing geometry: Suitable camera positioning to manage reflections and resolve targets.
  • Documentation: Thermal images, measurements, site conditions, and findings associated with identifiable assets.

These practices help separate genuine thermal abnormalities from artifacts caused by unsuitable inspection conditions.

INSPECTION METHODOLOGY

Measurement Quality Depends on More Than Camera Resolution

ThermaVista considers operating conditions, equipment characteristics, and environmental influences when acquiring and interpreting thermal data.

System Loading

Electrical heating is load-dependent. Equipment must operate under conditions suitable for the inspection objective.

Emissivity

Surface emissivity affects apparent temperature. Measurement parameters must account for material and surface characteristics.

Reflected Radiation

Reflective electrical surfaces can produce misleading apparent temperatures that require careful evaluation.

Temperature Comparison

Similar components under comparable operating conditions can provide useful reference temperatures and ΔT measurements.

Viewing Geometry

Distance, viewing angle, focus, and spatial resolution affect measurement reliability and anomaly detection.

Environmental Conditions

Ambient temperature, solar heating, wind, and weather can change the observed thermal response of equipment.

SYSTEM LIFECYCLE

The Right Inspection at the Right Time

Electrical commissioning, acceptance, and maintenance serve different objectives. Thermography can support each stage when appropriately specified and performed.

1 Install Construction and assembly
2 Commission ANSI/NETA ECS
3 Accept ANSI/NETA ATS
4 Operate Establish baseline condition
5 Maintain ANSI/NETA MTS

Thermographic findings support engineering, commissioning, and maintenance decisions; they do not replace required electrical tests.

DOCUMENTED RESULTS

Reports should allow maintenance and commissioning personnel to identify the affected equipment, understand the observation, and determine the appropriate next step.

Thermal Evidence

ELECTRICAL SAFETY

Safety Is Part of the Inspection Method

Energized electrical equipment presents electrical shock, arc-flash, and other hazards. Thermal imaging does not eliminate these risks.

INSPECTION LIMITATIONS

Appropriate Access, Qualified Personnel, and Defined Responsibilities

Thermographic inspection of energized equipment requires coordination with the facility's electrical safety program.

  • Access to energized equipment must be evaluated under applicable electrical safety requirements.
  • Removal of electrical covers or barriers may require qualified electrical personnel.
  • Arc-flash and shock hazards must be evaluated before exposure.
  • PPE, approach boundaries, permits, and work practices must be addressed as applicable.
  • Thermography cannot reveal every electrical defect, particularly where components are obstructed or operating conditions are unsuitable.

PROFESSIONAL REPRESENTATION

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Standards & Thermography FAQ

What is IEC TS 62446-3?

IEC TS 62446-3 is a technical specification addressing outdoor infrared thermography of operating photovoltaic modules and plants, including inspection equipment, environmental conditions, procedures, reporting, personnel qualifications, and guidance for classifying thermal abnormalities.

What is the difference between ANSI/NETA ECS and ATS?

ECS addresses the broader electrical commissioning process, while ATS focuses on acceptance testing and evaluation of electrical power equipment and systems. Both may apply to a new installation depending on project requirements.

Which standard applies to an existing electrical system?

ANSI/NETA MTS addresses maintenance testing of electrical equipment already in service. IEC TS 62446-3 may also be applicable to outdoor infrared inspection of operating photovoltaic systems.

Does thermography replace other electrical testing?

No. Thermal imaging is a complementary condition assessment method. Other electrical testing, visual inspections, functional verification, and commissioning activities may be required to establish equipment performance and compliance.

Can thermography identify a loose connection?

Abnormal heating may be consistent with a high-resistance connection, but thermography alone generally cannot prove the specific mechanical or electrical root cause. Further evaluation may be required.

Are inspections performed while equipment is operating?

Many thermographic inspections require equipment to operate under meaningful load. Conditions and safety procedures must be appropriate for the equipment and inspection objectives.
