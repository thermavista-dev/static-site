SEE CONDITIONS BEFORE THEY BECOME FAILURES

Increased Electrical Resistance Produces Heat

Electrical defects often develop gradually. A connection may loosen, corrode, deteriorate, or become overloaded while remaining visually unremarkable.

As resistance increases, heat can develop at the affected point. Infrared thermography allows these temperature differences to be identified while equipment is operating and carrying load.

By comparing similar components, phases, terminations, and operating conditions, thermography can help identify where additional investigation is warranted before a condition progresses into equipment damage or an outage.

WHAT THERMOGRAPHY CAN REVEAL

Abnormal Heat Can Point to Developing Electrical Conditions

Thermography identifies unusual thermal behavior. Findings are interpreted in context and typically require follow-up by qualified electrical personnel to determine root cause.

Thermal Observation Possible Condition Why It Matters Typical Follow-Up
Single hot termination Increased resistance, loose connection, corrosion, contamination, or contact degradation Resistive heating can worsen under load and may damage conductors or equipment Qualified electrical inspection and torque/connection evaluation
One phase hotter than adjacent phases Load imbalance, connection issue, conductor problem, or abnormal resistance Phase-to-phase comparison can reveal conditions that absolute temperature alone may not identify Load measurement and electrical testing
Hot circuit breaker High load, internal contact condition, poor terminal connection, or device deterioration May indicate abnormal heating within or around the breaker Load verification and breaker/termination evaluation
Fuse or fuse-holder heating Poor contact, damaged fuse clip, overload, or fuse deterioration Localized heating can affect reliability and component life Electrical inspection and component evaluation
Hot bus connection Increased resistance at bolted joint or connection interface High-current bus connections can generate substantial heat when contact resistance increases Qualified electrical inspection and corrective maintenance
Transformer termination hotspot Loose or degraded primary/secondary connection May indicate a developing high-resistance connection Electrical investigation and testing
Abnormal conductor heating Overload, undersized conductor, poor connection, harmonic loading, or imbalance Elevated conductor temperature can accelerate insulation aging Load analysis and conductor/circuit evaluation

Thermography identifies temperature patterns and abnormal conditions. It does not, by itself, prove a specific electrical root cause.

ΔT

Temperature Difference

The difference between the temperature of a component and an appropriate reference point can provide useful information about abnormal electrical heating.

WHY COMPARISON MATTERS

Absolute Temperature Is Only Part of the Story

Electrical thermography is often based on comparison rather than looking at a single temperature reading in isolation.

Useful comparisons may include:

  • Phase-to-phase temperature differences
  • Similar breakers carrying comparable loads
  • Adjacent terminations
  • Similar equipment operating under similar conditions
  • Component temperature relative to ambient conditions

Load, emissivity, reflected temperature, equipment type, accessibility, viewing angle, and environmental conditions all affect interpretation.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Electrical Thermography FAQ

Does the equipment need to be energized during inspection?

Generally, yes. Electrical thermography is most useful when equipment is operating under representative load because current flow is what produces the heating associated with many electrical abnormalities.

Can thermography prove that a connection is loose?

Not by itself. Thermography can identify a thermal pattern consistent with increased resistance or an abnormal connection, but mechanical or electrical evaluation is typically required to confirm the root cause.

Can switchgear be inspected while energized?

Inspection depends on safe access, equipment design, electrical-safety requirements, site procedures, and the inspection scope. Open-panel or energized-equipment inspection must be coordinated with qualified personnel and the applicable electrical safety program.

How often should electrical equipment be thermally inspected?

Frequency depends on equipment criticality, loading, environment, maintenance history, owner requirements, and applicable maintenance programs. Higher-consequence equipment may justify more frequent condition monitoring.

Is thermography only useful for old equipment?

No. New equipment can exhibit installation-related problems, poor connections, load imbalance, or component defects. Thermography can therefore be useful during commissioning as well as throughout the maintenance lifecycle.

What does ThermaVista provide after the inspection?

Typical deliverables include thermal and visible imagery, equipment identification, temperatures, temperature differences, finding descriptions, priorities, and recommended follow-up.
