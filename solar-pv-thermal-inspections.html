SEE WHAT NORMAL IMAGERY MISSES

A Solar Module Can Look Normal and Still Have a Problem

Many photovoltaic defects are difficult or impossible to identify through conventional visual inspection alone. Under appropriate operating conditions, defective cells, electrical connections, bypass components, and other abnormalities can produce distinct thermal patterns.

Radiometric infrared imaging allows those temperature differences to be measured, mapped, and compared across large PV installations. Drone-based inspection makes that process practical even across hundreds or thousands of modules.

ThermaVista combines aerial imaging with targeted ground thermography to provide both broad system coverage and detailed inspection of electrical equipment.

WHAT THERMAL IMAGING CAN REVEAL

Thermal Patterns Provide Clues About System Condition

Infrared thermography identifies abnormal thermal behavior. The resulting patterns can help direct electrical testing, troubleshooting, maintenance, and corrective action.

Thermal Observation Possible Condition Why It Matters Typical Follow-Up
Individual hot cell Cell damage, mismatch, cracking, localized shading, or other module abnormality May affect module performance and could indicate a developing failure mechanism Visual inspection, electrical testing, module evaluation
Substring heating Bypass diode or substring-related condition May indicate reduced module output or abnormal current flow Electrical evaluation and module troubleshooting
Entire module warmer than adjacent modules Open circuit, wiring issue, operating mismatch, or module-level electrical condition May indicate loss of production or abnormal module operation String-level electrical testing and inspection
Connector hotspot Increased resistance, poor contact, contamination, improper assembly, or connection degradation Resistive heating may increase over time and warrants investigation Qualified electrical inspection and corrective evaluation
Junction-box heating Internal connection, diode, or component abnormality May indicate an electrical issue not visible externally Targeted inspection and electrical diagnostics
String-level thermal difference Current imbalance, open string, shading, mismatch, or electrical fault May correspond with reduced production from an affected string String current and voltage testing
Combiner or fuse heating Resistive connection, fuse condition, imbalance, or conductor issue Could affect reliability and electrical-system performance Ground-based thermography and qualified electrical evaluation
Inverter termination hotspot Abnormal resistance at a conductor or terminal connection High-current connections can produce significant heating when resistance increases Qualified electrician investigation and corrective action

Thermal signatures identify abnormal temperature patterns and potential conditions; they do not necessarily establish root cause without appropriate follow-up evaluation.

INSPECTION CONDITIONS MATTER

Thermal Data Is Only as Good as the Conditions Under Which It Was Collected

PV thermal inspection depends upon meaningful electrical loading and sufficiently stable environmental conditions. These factors are evaluated and documented as part of the inspection.

Solar Irradiance Adequate and reasonably stable sunlight is necessary to create useful thermal contrast across operating PV modules.
Wind Excessive airflow can cool modules and electrical components, reducing or altering observable temperature differences.
Cloud Cover Rapid changes in irradiance can create transient temperature patterns that complicate interpretation.
System Load Electrical abnormalities are more readily observed when the equipment is operating under meaningful load.
Viewing Angle Inspection geometry must limit reflections and provide sufficient image detail for reliable interpretation.
Spatial Resolution Flight altitude, optics, sensor resolution, and target size determine how much module-level detail can be resolved.
Surface Effects Soiling, shadows, vegetation, moisture, and reflections can produce thermal patterns that require contextual interpretation.
Documentation Environmental and operating conditions provide essential context for interpreting the resulting thermographic data.
IEC TS 62446-3

Photovoltaic Systems — Outdoor Infrared Thermography

ThermaVista's PV inspection methodology is aligned with applicable principles and practices described in IEC TS 62446-3 for outdoor infrared thermography of photovoltaic modules and plants.

WHY THE STANDARD MATTERS

Consistent Procedures Produce More Defensible Results

IEC TS 62446-3 provides a framework for performing and documenting outdoor infrared thermographic inspections of operating PV systems. It addresses important elements of the inspection process, including:

  • Infrared inspection equipment
  • Environmental conditions during inspection
  • PV system operating conditions
  • Image acquisition procedures
  • Inspection geometry and image quality
  • Evaluation of thermal abnormalities
  • Reporting requirements
  • Personnel considerations and inspection methodology

The objective is not merely to capture attractive infrared photographs. It is to obtain thermographic data under controlled enough conditions that the results can support meaningful engineering, commissioning, and maintenance decisions.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Solar PV Thermal Inspection FAQ

Can thermal imaging identify defective solar panels?

Thermal imaging can identify abnormal temperature patterns associated with certain module, cell, substring, connection, shading, and operating conditions. Thermography is generally used to locate and characterize anomalies; additional electrical or physical testing may be required to determine definitive root cause.

Why use a drone instead of inspecting modules by hand?

Commercial photovoltaic installations can contain thousands of modules spread across rooftops, carports, or ground-mounted arrays. A thermal drone can inspect large areas efficiently while collecting thermal and visible imagery with geographic context. Ground inspection remains valuable for electrical equipment and targeted follow-up.

Does the PV system need to be operating during inspection?

Yes. Meaningful PV thermography generally requires the system to be operating under sufficient solar irradiance and electrical load. Environmental and system conditions are therefore an important part of inspection planning.

Can an inspection be performed on a cloudy day?

Variable clouds can rapidly alter module temperature and irradiance, reducing the reliability of thermal comparisons. Suitable inspection windows are selected based on environmental conditions and the objectives of the inspection.

Should a new solar installation receive a thermal inspection?

Thermal inspection can provide useful commissioning information once the system is energized and operating. It may reveal abnormal conditions not apparent during conventional visual inspection and can establish a baseline for subsequent maintenance inspections.

How often should an existing PV system be thermally inspected?

Inspection frequency depends on asset criticality, operating environment, maintenance strategy, equipment history, owner requirements, and applicable maintenance programs. Many owners incorporate thermography into periodic condition-based maintenance rather than relying solely on one-time inspection.

What is included in the inspection report?

Typical reports include inspection scope, methodology, environmental conditions, thermal and visible imagery, equipment identification, finding locations, temperature observations, anomaly descriptions, priorities, and recommended follow-up.

Does ThermaVista repair the problems it identifies?

ThermaVista's primary role is inspection, thermographic analysis, documentation, and condition assessment. Findings can be provided to the owner's electrician, EPC contractor, O&M provider, equipment manufacturer, or other qualified service provider for corrective work.
